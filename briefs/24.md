## Part 1: Spanish and German number words (`anamnesis/providers/stated.py`, `anamnesis/providers/numbers.py`)
1. **Tables keyed by `catalogue.normalise` output** (casefolded, accents and umlauts kept, ß as ss), as for English and Arabic. Each Spanish word is accepted with and without its accents ("veintitrés", "veintitres").
2. **Spanish:**
   - the words in SPEC §2;
   - `cien` and `ciento` are a **plain 100**, never a hundred multiplier;
   - `doscientos`–`novecientos` and their `-as` forms are plain values;
   - `mil` is a thousand word;
   - `medio` and `media` are a half;
   - `once` (11) is left out, because it is the English "once", which states nothing;
   - `cien`, `ciento` and `doscientos`–`novecientos` close a run that already holds a number: "cinco cien" states 5 and 100, never 105 or 500.
   - **`y` keeps a run open only** between a ten (`treinta`–`noventa`) and a following 1–9 value, or before `medio`/`media`. Any other `y` ends the run, so "diez y cincuenta" states 10 and 50, never 60.
3. **German:**
   - **One helper reads a whole token** as a number up to 9,999: an optional `tausend`, then an optional `hundert`, each after an optional multiplier `ein`–`neun`, then a unit, a teen, a ten, or a unit plus `und` plus a ten.
   - **The halves:** `einhalb` is a half (0.5). `eineinhalb` is 1.5 and `zweieinhalb`–`neuneinhalb` are n + 0.5.
   - **`hundert` and `tausend` on their own** are a hundred and a thousand word, as in English, so "fünf hundert" is 500. A one-word German number from 100 to 999 closes a run that already holds a number, as the Spanish hundreds do.
   - **The whole token must be consumed,** or it states nothing: "Achtung" and "Einheit" state nothing, and "zweieinhalb" states 2.5 only, never 1.
   - `halb`, `halbe`, `halben`, `halber` and `halbes` are a half.
4. **The articles:**
   - Spanish `un` and `una`, and German `ein`, `eine`, `einen`, `einem` and `einer`, count as 1 only when the next token is a unit word (SPEC §2 lists them), or, in Spanish, when `y medio` or `y media` follows.
   - Otherwise they end the run like English `a`.
   - An article that counts can join a run: "treinta y un miligramos" states 31.
   - `uno` and `eins` always count as 1.
5. **`coma` and `komma` are the decimal point,** like `point`: the digit words after them may be English, Spanish or German.
6. **`zweimal`, `dreimal` and the like** are not in any table, so they state nothing.
7. **`parse_amount_text`** reads a single Spanish or German number word like its digits, as it reads English and Arabic ones: "quinientos", "fünfzig", "fünfhundertfünfundsiebzig", "zweieinhalb". Articles never parse.
8. **English and Arabic behave exactly as before.** The existing tests in `tests/contract/test_b20_*`, `test_b21_*`, `test_b22_*` and `test_b23_*` must stay green.

## Part 2: the claim parser (`anamnesis/providers/claims.py`, `parse_claims_json`)
9. **One small helper decides whether an ingredient field is grounded** by the turns a claim cites, exactly as SPEC §2 says:
   - after `catalogue.normalise`, it is a run of whole words in a cited turn (Arabic words may carry `catalogue.ARABIC_CLITICS`, the same boundary rule as `_says_release` in `reconcile.py`);
   - or each of its words, with one final vowel removed when it has 6 letters or more, begins a word of a cited turn.
   - A field made only of digits, unit words or number words is never grounded. Use `stated_amounts` and the unit words to tell.
   - **An amount label only** is also grounded when its `canonical_inn` is an ingredient of the catalogue entry the claim's `name_text` resolves to. So Augmentin's "amoxicillin" and "clavulanic acid", and Concor's "Bisoprolol fumarate", are kept as the model wrote them. The claim's own `inn` is grounded by the turns only.
   - **Why the catalogue route matters:** the CEO's reference build shows that grounding labels by the turns alone breaks `tests/contract/test_b8_claims.py::test_parse_splits_salts_through_the_schema`, `tests/contract/test_b21_media_fixes.py::test_a_combination_strength_said_in_words_is_kept` and `tests/unit/test_stated_extra.py::test_claim_strength_kept_only_when_every_ingredient_is_stated`. Two labels relabelled to the same brand make the strength invalid.
   - **`parse_claims_json(text, turns, catalogue=None)`** takes an optional catalogue. Without one, it uses the bundled catalogue, loaded once. Every existing two-argument call keeps working.
10. **The claim's `inn`** becomes null when it isn't grounded.
11. **Before an amount is validated,** an empty or ungrounded label (`intake_amount.inn`, and each strength ingredient's `inn`) is replaced by the claim's grounded `inn`, or, when there is none, by the claim's `name_text`. Relabelling never drops an amount.
12. **A `strength` whose `ingredients` is an empty list becomes null without counting as a dropped amount:** the rule "a dropped claim amount takes the other one with it" must not fire for it. Every other drop still takes the other amount with it (`test_an_unstated_amount_still_takes_the_other_with_it`).
