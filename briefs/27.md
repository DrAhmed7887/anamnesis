# Relation checks: an amount must be said about the claim it grounds (SPEC §2)

You are the builder for batch 27 of Anamnesis. Read SPEC.md §2, "Relation checks (batch 27)", first: it is the specification. The contract tests are `tests/contract/test_b27_relations.py`.

## Why
- **The gap:** `parse_claims_json` (`anamnesis/providers/claims.py`, around line 349) keeps an amount when its value is anywhere in `stated_amounts` of a cited turn. That proves the number was said, not what it was said about.
- **What it lets through:** a reply that takes "ten, not five" as 5 mg, "five micrograms" as 5 mg, "two Panadol tablets" as 2 mg, another medicine's "five hundred", a past dose, or a daily total split into two doses. Three of these become a false `match` in the brief.
- **What must not change:** the real models' replies already pass. The rules in the SPEC were measured on them before this brief was written, and they drop no amount a careful listener keeps.

## Build
1. **The rules** go in a new module, `anamnesis/providers/relations.py`. Suggested shape (yours to change):
   - a tokenizer for one turn that keeps a digit run with its decimal point or comma as one token, `normalise`s every other word, and marks clause ends;
   - a function that returns the mentions a cited turn gives one claim: each value of each number run with its unit (or none) and whether it is a daily total, after rules 1–5;
   - a function that says whether an amount (value, unit, intake or strength) is grounded by a claim's mentions, with rule 6 for intakes.
2. **The hook** is in `parse_claims_json`, right after the existing value check (`said`):
   - for each claim, an `intake_amount` or a strength ingredient amount that the relation checks don't ground is dropped exactly as an unstated one is, so the existing rule then drops the other amount too;
   - the claim's own names come from its raw dict (`name_text`, `inn`, its amounts' labels) and the catalogue entry `name_text` resolves to; other medicines' names come from the catalogue and from the other claims in the same reply.
3. **Reuse what exists:** `normalise` and `ARABIC_CLITICS` (`anamnesis/core/catalogue.py`), `has_number_role`, `stated_amounts`, `GATE_WORDS` and `SPANISH_DOSE_WORDS` (`anamnesis/providers/stated.py`), and `Catalogue.resolve` and `.entries`. Keep the SPEC's word lists as module constants, each with a SPEC reference.
4. **Your own tests** go in `tests/unit/test_b27_extra.py`.
