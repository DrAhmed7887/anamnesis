## Words we never use
- "autonomous", "self-correcting", "reorients", "plans its own steps": the order is fixed in code and no model chooses an action (SPEC.md:349). For the follow-up, say "runs the follow-up by itself" (row 2.7).
- "25 languages": that is Voice Transcribe's own range. Anamnesis ran live on English and Egyptian Arabic, plus eight Spanish and German notes in batch 24 and the Spanish hero's 30 runs, all synthetic voices (SPEC.md:3; rows 4.27, 4.30).
- "works in Spanish", "Spanish-ready" or "German-ready": Spanish ran on one synthetic voice, in eight notes and the Spanish hero's three sets, and German on four notes. Say the counts (rows 4.27, 4.30).
- "validated", "clinically tested", "prevents errors", "saves N minutes", "caught a double dose", "catches it": nothing measured these. The runs on public photos are staged tests (docs/DATA_HANDLING.md:49).
- "real-world", "real patients" or "in the wild" for any staged case: the cases use public photos of real packs and synthetic voices (row 3.3). "Real" is allowed only for what is real: a public photo of a real pack, or a transcript Voice Transcribe really returned.
- "not a medical device", or any regulatory class: nothing in the repo assesses Anamnesis's regulatory status. Say "Anamnesis gives no medical advice, and every run so far is a staged test, not clinical use" (row 1.15).
- "never shows advice": pack text is quoted as printed and can include directions (SPEC.md:48). Say "adds no advice of its own".
- "open source" for the repository or the product: the code is Apache-2.0, and that licence covers the code only; the data it uses stay under their own terms (row 6.8). Say "the code is licensed under Apache-2.0".
- "MD", or anything that implies current practice. Say "physician (MBBCh)".
- "proven", "verified" or "formally checked" for the code, "formal invariants", and a test count presented as invariants or as progress: the tests are tests, and the count is whatever the suite gives (2,757 at the tag, row 5.7; 2,809 on `main` after batch 31, row 5.19). The mutants and the differential checks test the contract and compare two builds of one SPEC (row 5.11).
- "guarantee" or "guaranteed", "firewall", "fail-closed", "fails closed" or "fails safe" as a promise or as a property of the system: nothing guarantees a finding, and a fail-open is on record (row 1.19). Say what happened in a case, for example "the amount was dropped and the finding was 'unconfirmed'". Say that code, not a model, does the comparison (row 1.16).
- "eliminates hallucination", "zero hallucination": the checks drop amounts and ingredients that a turn or a label doesn't support (rows 1.13, 1.22, 1.23), and nothing measured hallucination as such. "Zero outputs scored harmful under our stated rubric in these runs" is allowed, with the run.
- "independent reviewer", "independent validation", or "Muse couldn't edit the tests": the reviewer is a separate Claude Opus subagent of the same Claude Code session, the auditor a fresh Muse session, and Muse wasn't allowed to edit the tests (rows 5.4, 5.15).
- "FHIR", "EHR-ready", "interoperable", "digital twin": no export, integration or twin exists.
- "95.7%", or any accuracy percentage, for the fine-tune without its scope: it is Glimmer + LoRA r1, 290 of 303 fully right on synthetic held-out test notes, an opt-in extractor (row 4.18). Prefer the counts.
- "the product is fine-tuned", "runs Glimmer" or "runs on open weights" for the default path: the default path is Meta-hosted Muse Spark 1.3 for the claims and the labels (row 1.21).
- "better", "beats", "outperforms", "matches" and "as good as", for any comparison of 16 cases or fewer. Every U-case column is 8 cases, or 8 × 2 runs. For the 303 test notes, say the counts and what the tuned model learned (row 4.18). For a label reader, say "descriptive comparison, n = 20" (row 4.13).
- A hero total across sets ("21 runs", "0 of 21", "31 runs"): the four sets ran on four versions of the code and are never added together. Say "9 of 10 on the final code", and each other set with its own code (rows 4.5, 4.22, 4.28, 4.29). "0 harmful in each set" is fine. The Spanish hero's three sets are never added together either ("16 of 30"): say "9 of 10 in the third set", with 3 and 4 of 10 for the first two (row 4.30).
- "verified against PubChem" on its own: say "35 of 52 aliases agree with PubChem, none disagree, 17 aren't listed there" (row 6.7).
- Any statistic or citation without a row here.
